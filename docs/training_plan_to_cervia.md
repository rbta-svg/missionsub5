## Session priorities

Two weeks of data show a clear pattern: she completes long rides and races, and skips structured work, gym and swims. So each session is marked.

- **A — never skip.** If the week collapses, these survive.
- **B — move it, don't bin it.** Push to another day if needed.
- **C — nice to have.** Losing one costs nothing.

If she does only the A sessions every week, she still arrives in Cervia ready.

Three to four runs a week. Two swims. Two to three rides.

**Two rules that override everything:**

1. **Every ride over an hour goes in the aero bars.** The position is new and needs to ache in August, not on race day.

**Effort by *RPE* out of 10 and by heart rate.** Easy = 4-5. Steady = 5-6. Tempo = 6-7. **Bike race effort = HR 155-160, RPE 6.** Run race effort = 4:35/km.

**How to read a swim set.** "6 × 200" means swim 200 metres, rest, and repeat six times. Rest between each is written next to it. Everything else is spelled out in sentences.

# Glossary

### Swim words

**"6 × 200"** — swim 200 metres, rest, repeat six times. The rest is written next to it.

**Kick** — legs only, no arms. Either holding a kickboard out in front, or with both arms stretched ahead. It builds leg strength and, more importantly, teaches her to hold a flat body position instead of letting the hips sink.

**Build** — get faster *within* a single repeat. Start easy at the beginning, finish fast at the end. Different from "each one faster than the last", which applies across repeats.

**Catch-up drill** — one arm stays stretched out in front until the other hand touches it, then they swap. It slows the stroke right down and forces a long, patient reach instead of a hurried one.

**Pull buoy** — a float held between the thighs. The legs stop working, so the body position has to be held by the core and arms alone. Teaches a flat, still shape in the water.

**Paddles** — plastic plates strapped to the hands. More surface area means more resistance, which teaches the forearm to hold water properly rather than slipping through it. Use sparingly — they load the shoulders.

**Fins** — short swim fins. They add propulsion so a drill can be done slowly and correctly without sinking.

**Sighting** — lifting the eyes forward every few strokes to check direction. There are no lane ropes in open water, and swimming 5% off course adds two minutes to her race. The cheapest skill in the sport.

**Her test pace** — worked out from the 400 m and 200 m time trials on 3 August. Wherever a set says "at her test pace", that is the number.

### Effort

**RPE** — how hard it feels, 1 to 10, nothing measured. 4 = could talk in full sentences all day. 6 = short sentences only. 8 = single words. 10 = sprint.

**Easy** (RPE 4-5) — conversational. Most training lives here and should feel almost too gentle. This is where endurance is built.

**Steady** (RPE 5-6) — comfortably working. Long rides and long runs.

**Tempo** (RPE 6-7) — sustainably uncomfortable. Could hold it an hour, wouldn't want to.

**Race effort** — bike: heart rate 155-160, RPE 6. Run: 4:35/km.

### Running

**Fartlek** — Swedish for "speed play". Faster and easier blocks alternating inside one continuous run, without stopping. Teaches the legs to change gear and recover while still moving.

**Progressive run** — each section faster than the last. Finishes strong on tired legs, which is exactly what a half marathon off the bike demands.

**Pyramid intervals** — repeats that grow then shrink (200, 400, 800, 400, 200). Covers a range of speeds in one session.

**Brick** — a bike immediately followed by a run with no real gap. The legs feel heavy and strange for the first ten minutes; the point is to make that feeling familiar rather than alarming.

### Cycling

**Aero position / in the bars** — forearms resting on the aero bars, torso low, head down. Cuts air resistance by roughly a sixth, worth several minutes over 90 km at no extra effort. It uses different muscles and needs practising.

**Cadence** — pedal revolutions per minute. 85-95 is the target: spinning lighter gears saves the legs for the run.

**Bike fit** — a professional session adjusting saddle, bars and position. On a road bike with clip-on aero bars this matters more than usual, because the standard frame geometry fights the aero position.

**Power meter** — measures effort in watts, unaffected by wind, hills or mood. Hers is reading nonsense, so heart rate is the governor instead.
