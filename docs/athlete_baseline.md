## The shape of the training

98 days, 81 of them with training, never more than three days off. Roughly 143 hours, 9.5 h/week on average, peaking at 15.4 h in the week of 29 June. Ten sessions of strength, pilates and HIIT round out the swim/bike/run work — about one a week.

The split by hours: bike 62% (88.6 h, 1,951 km), run 20% (29.3 h, 317 km), swim 13% (18.5 h, 47.5 km), other 5%. That is a cyclist's distribution wearing a triathlete's calendar.

## Discipline by discipline

**Swim** — the weak leg, and the fastest improving. Pool pace for swims over 1,500 m moved from ~2:30–2:45/100m in early April to ~2:05–2:15/100m by June. Best pool session: 2:01/100m. One open-water swim logged at 1:42/100m (wetsuit and GPS optimism both likely). Two swims a week, 2–3 km each, longest 3,050 m.

**Bike** — the engine. Regular 60–150 km rides at 24–27 km/h solo, including a 173 km day in early May and a 340 km week. Recent 50–70 km rides sit at 25–27 km/h. Little visible structure: almost no power files, no interval signature in the session data. Big diesel, unquantified top end.

**Run** — steady and honest. Three to four runs a week, typical quality pace 5:00–5:20/km, a 10 km at 4:37/km in mid-June, and a longest run of 19.8 km at 5:08/km on 9 July. Only two runs beyond 15 km in the whole block — run durability is the open question.

## Flags

- Several same-day near-duplicate ride files (dual-device recording) may slightly inflate bike volume.
- TSS/IF and power are missing from most files; load must be judged by hours, not stress scores.
- The zones data in the files is inconsistent (FTP values swing 148–338 W between files). Treat 195 W as a guess, not a measurement.
