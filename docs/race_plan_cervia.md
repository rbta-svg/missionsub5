## The error I made, and the correction

I anchored her FTP at 205 W, taken from the June Olympic race file (NP 202 W over 73 minutes) on the assumption that was a near-maximal effort. Modelling last year's 33.69 km/h on the aluminium bike gives ~196 W, which against a 205 W FTP is **IF 0.96**. Nobody rides ninety-six percent of threshold for two hours forty. Rather than reject the anchor, I accepted the absurd number and built a plan around riding *slower* than last year — concluding that a carbon bike with aero bars was worth nothing. That was wrong.

Back-solving from a defensible race intensity of 0.85-0.88 puts her **FTP at roughly 225 W**. Everything then coheres: last year reads as IF 0.87, hard and slightly hot, which the 5:14/km run confirms. The June Olympic at NP 202 becomes IF 0.90 over 73 minutes — a ride with something held back, consistent with the 4:37/km she ran off it.

**Lesson for the model: when a derived number is physiologically impossible, the input is wrong, not the athlete.**

## The bike, calibrated against the measured 205 W

Her Cervia bike leg was **205 W average**. That is a measurement, not a model, and it pins her 2025 aerodynamics exactly. Solving the power equation backwards for 33.69 km/h over 90 km and 304 m:

**CdA 2025 = 0.292 m²** — worse than the 0.275 I had assumed, which means the equipment gain is *larger* than my previous estimate, not smaller.

Also worth stating plainly: 205 W average sustained for two hours forty is **3.25 W/kg**. She is a strong cyclist and I have been under-rating her throughout.

### Now apply the strategy on top

Back off to **IF 0.82 — 195 W, ten watts *less* than last year** — on the helmet-and-fit package:

**35.35 km/h, 2:32:44. Seven minutes faster than 2025 on less power.** Easier and quicker simultaneously. That is the whole argument, and the measured wattage makes it stronger than my earlier version did.

**Race-day instruction: hold 195 W, HR 150-156, ceiling 162.** Let speed be the output, not the target. Do not chase a number on the Bertinoro drag — power is the governor.

### Where the 3:10 came from

Against the previous 5:02:55, the shaving is deliberately lopsided:

- **Run −1:32.** 4:40 → 4:36/km. The largest slice, and the only one that is genuinely trainable in six weeks.
- **Bike −37s.** 34.4 → 34.5 km/h, moving from IF 0.80 to 0.81. Trivial, and below the Karlsfelder effort.
- **Swim −30s.** 38:30 → 38:00, which is exactly her Karlsfelder open-water pace of 2:00/100m.
- **Transitions −30s.** 8:45 → 8:15, against a winner's 8:00 and her own 4:36 at Karlsfelder.

I resisted taking more from the bike. The intensity curve is flat — pushing the bike harder buys minutes on paper and gives them back on the run, with a fat downside tail if it goes wrong. The run is where sub-5 is actually won.

### What has to be true

**Run 4:36/km for 21.1 km off the bike.** She ran 4:26/km for 9.8 km off a 45 km bike at Karlsfelder, at HR 173. This asks for 10 s/km slower over double the distance off double the bike. Demanding, not fanciful — and it is exactly what the long-run block is for.

**Bike 34.5 km/h at HR 155-160.** Below the 168 she held for 77 minutes at Karlsfelder. The speed comes from the aero package, not from working harder.

**Transitions 8:15.** On a course where the winner took 8:00 — but she did 4:36 at Karlsfelder, so the skill is there. This is about knowing the route, not moving faster.

**A straight swim.** 2:00/100m is her real pace. Swimming wide is what costs the minute, which is why open-water sighting is now a protected session.

**Helmet bought and fit done by late August.** Without them the bike is 2:40 and the target is gone.

### The plan changed to earn it

Race-pace volume inside the long runs roughly doubled: 4 km, then 5, then **6 km at 4:36/km** inside the 21 km. Interval pace dropped from 4:35 to 4:28/km. The full rehearsal run went from 30 to 40 minutes. Both bricks now finish with a **timed T2 under two minutes**, because transitions cannot be drilled for the first time in race week.

## Two open problems

**The power data conflicts.** 205 W average at Cervia last September against 192 W avg / 202 NP in the June 2026 Olympic. If her FTP is ~238 W, that June ride was IF 0.85 — soft for an Olympic-distance race. Either she was fitter last September than she is now, or the two bikes' power meters disagree. **An FTP test in week one is the most valuable missing number in this project.**

**The 2026 CdA figures are modelled, not measured.** The 205 W anchors 2025 precisely; the aero-gain percentages are textbook. Clip-ons bolted to a road frame can disappoint — the seat angle is wrong and the hips stay closed, so the theoretical gain is not always realised.

**Test it rather than assume it:** a flat out-and-back, exactly 190 W held in the bars, measure the average speed, and the real CdA falls straight out of the equation. One session converts the largest number in this plan from an assumption into a measurement.

## Priorities, by return on effort

1. **Aero helmet + bike fit** — 4 to 7 minutes, purchasable this fortnight. The single best return available.
2. **Transitions** — 6:16, free. Two drills and a full rehearsal with the clock running.
3. **Run** — 8 to 10 minutes, trainable. The long-run build, every brick off a *controlled* bike.
4. **Swim** — about a minute. Maintenance only; the hours are worth more elsewhere.

## Fix before race day

Her July power files read 28-63 W at 26 km/h — a broken meter or a second bike without one. This plan is governed by watts. She needs working power on race day and in training from now, or the pacing instruction is unenforceable.

## What this means for the next six weeks

**The run is the only leg with trainable time in it.** Ten minutes sit there. Long runs at 15/17/19/21 km with race-pace finishes, and every brick run executed off a *controlled* bike, never a hard one.

**The swim goes to maintenance.** Two sessions a week, technique and open-water sighting, no volume chase. Pool pace has been flat at 2:03-2:11 since May and six weeks will not move it. Spending run hours on it would be a net loss. Fix the start position and the sighting instead — that is worth more than any set.

**Bike training is not about watts.** It is about two things: adapting to the aero position, and rehearsing restraint. If she has not done three-hour rides in the bars, the position will hurt at 60 km and her run will pay. Every long ride from here goes in the bars, at 164 W / HR 148, holding back on the hills. Riding slowly on purpose is a skill and it needs practice.

**Transitions get drilled.** Seven free minutes, more than any session in the next six weeks will yield. Two dedicated sessions plus a full swim-T1-bike-T2-run rehearsal with the clock running.

## Caveats

The CdA figures are modelled, not measured, and calibrate cleanly against her June power file (model 182 W, file 192 W avg / 202 NP) — but they are estimates. The run-penalty curve rests on a single anchor point. And Cervia is a notoriously congested bike course; some of last year's 33.69 km/h may have come from packs rather than legs, which would mean the 196 W figure overstates her true output. None of it changes the recommendation.
