**T2 — 5:10.** Helmet off, shoes on, go.

**Run — 1:36:55.** First 3 km at 4:40/km, and it will feel too easy. Run them anyway. Then 4:35/km. From 15 km, whatever is left.

## Why these numbers

### Last year, and what it cost

She rode 2:40:02 at **205 W average** — a measured figure, not a model — on an **aluminium road bike with no aero bars**. Solving the physics backwards for 33.69 km/h over 90 km and 304 m of climbing gives a drag area of **CdA 0.292**.

205 W held for two hours forty is **3.25 W/kg**. She is a strong cyclist, and the 5:14/km run afterwards says she spent too much of that strength on the bike course.

### Karlsfelder, 19 July — the best evidence we have

| | Bike | HR | Run | HR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 14 Jun Olympic | 32.70 km/h | 171 | 4:37 /km | 175 |
| **19 Jul Karlsfelder** | **35.27 km/h** | **168** | **4:26 /km** | **173** |

Both legs faster at lower heart rate, five weeks apart. That is a fitness gain, not a good day. Karlsfelder's terrain is near-identical to Cervia per kilometre, so it transfers.

Back-solving the bike leg gives roughly **210 W for 77 minutes at HR 168**, implying an **FTP near 225-230 W** — independently confirming the figure derived from last year's 205 W.

Transitions there were **T1 1:48, T2 2:48**. She can transition. Cervia's 11:46 was the course, not the athlete.

### The bike gain is equipment, not fitness

At the same effort — **IF 0.81, 184 W, HR 155-160**:

| Setup | CdA | Speed | Split |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 aluminium + hoods (measured) | 0.292 | 33.69 km/h | 2:40:02 |
| Bianchi + clip-ons | 0.254 | ~33.9 km/h | ~2:39 |
| + aero helmet + bike fit | 0.231 | 34.55 km/h | 2:36:18 |
| **+ skinsuit** | **0.219** | **35.08 km/h** | **2:33:55** |

**Six minutes faster than last year at lower effort than last year.** None of it comes from the legs.

### Transitions, and who pays for them

Transitions are set at **10:55 combined** — barely faster than last year, on the realistic view that Cervia's transitions are long by design. The winner took 8:00. Assuming she matches that would be planning on fiction.

That realism costs **2:40** against an optimistic transition assumption. Almost all of it is paid by the **skinsuit: 2:23 for the same watts.** The remaining 17 seconds falls on the run.

This is the cheapest trade in the plan — a slow transition bought back by a garment. **The skinsuit is mandatory, not optional.**

### Why the bike is not pushed harder

Modelling bike intensity from IF 0.70 to 0.94 gives a nearly flat total: about four minutes separates the extremes, ninety seconds between 0.78 and 0.86. Riding harder buys minutes on paper and hands them back on the run — with a fat downside tail, because real 70.3 blow-ups are a cliff rather than a gentle slope. Same expected time, far more variance. So the bike sits at IF 0.81 and the speed comes from drag reduction.

## What has to be true

**Skinsuit, aero helmet and bike fit all done by late August.** The skinsuit alone is worth 2:23. Hardest dependency in the plan, easiest to satisfy.

**Run 4:35/km for 21.1 km off the bike.** She ran 4:26/km for 9.8 km off a 45 km bike at Karlsfelder, at HR 173. This asks for 9 s/km slower over double the distance off double the bike. Demanding, not fanciful — and exactly what the long-run block is for.

**Bike 35.1 km/h at HR 155-160.** Below the 168 she held for 77 minutes at Karlsfelder, and the same effort as the earlier plan. The kit does the work.

**A straight swim.** 2:00/100m is her real open-water pace. Swimming wide costs the minute, which is why open-water sighting is a protected session.

## How the training plan earns it

Race-pace volume inside the long runs roughly doubled — 4 km, then 5, then **6 km at 4:35/km inside the 21 km**. Interval pace dropped to 4:28/km. The rehearsal run went from 30 to 40 minutes. Both bricks now finish with a **timed T2 under two minutes**, because transitions cannot be drilled for the first time in race week.

## Open problems

**The power data conflicts.** 205 W at Cervia last September against 192 W avg / 202 NP at the June Olympic. Either she was fitter last September, or the two bikes' meters disagree. An FTP test would settle it — suggested, not required, since the plan runs on heart rate regardless.

**The 2026 drag figures are modelled, not measured.** The 205 W anchors 2025 precisely; the aero-gain percentages are textbook. Clip-ons on a road frame can disappoint, because the seat angle is wrong and the hips stay closed.

**Test it rather than assume it:** a flat out-and-back at a fixed effort in the bars, measure the average speed, and the real drag falls straight out of the equation. One session converts the largest assumption in this plan into a measurement.

## Priorities, by return

1. **Skinsuit, aero helmet, bike fit** — about 6 minutes, purchasable. The best return available, and the only one with a deadline.
2. **Run** — about 14 minutes against last year. The long-run block with race-pace finishes.
3. **Transitions** — 1 minute. Worth drilling, not worth building the plan around.
4. **Swim** — 2 minutes, mostly from sighting rather than fitness. Maintenance only.
